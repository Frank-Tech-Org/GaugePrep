# Voice

GaugePrep speaks like a calm senior examiner who is on your side: **honest, precise, calm, encouraging**. Never harsh, cold, hyped or flattering. We write natively in English and French; neither is a translation of the other.

## Rules

- Say where the learner stands, in the exam's own scale, and how sure we are. Every prediction is labelled an estimate and shows a range.
- Plain words, short sentences. Speak to "you" ("vous" in French, never "tu" in product copy).
- Lead with the next useful step, not with praise.
- No exclamation marks in product copy. No "guaranteed", "100%", "pass for sure", "secret". No fear tactics about visas or deadlines.
- Sentence case everywhere: headings, buttons, menus. Brand name always "GaugePrep", one word, capital G and P.
- Exam names are descriptive only ("TCF Canada", "IELTS Academic"); no implied partnership.

## In practice

| Error | We couldn't save your answer. Check your connection; your work is kept on this device. | Nous n'avons pas pu enregistrer votre réponse. Vérifiez votre connexion ; votre travail est conservé sur cet appareil. |

## Avoid

- "Amazing job!!! You're crushing it 🎉" (hype, emoji).
- "You will pass TCF Canada." (a promise we cannot keep).
- "Only 3 days left to save your future." (fear).

## Tagline

The signature is **Know where you stand.** In French: **Sachez où vous en êtes.** It sits under the lockup, closes films and leads the website hero. Keep it exactly as written, with the full stop, in Fraunces on marketing pieces.

Campaign lines, used for single campaigns only, never as the signature: "Your exam, measured honestly." / "Votre examen, mesuré honnêtement." (the Measure campaign) and "Ready, for real." / "Prêt, pour de vrai." (the Rehearse campaign; use "Prête" when speaking to a woman, or rewrite neutrally).
